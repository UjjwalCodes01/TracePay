Field notes on AI coding assistant adoption, by independent analyst Mara Okafor. Synthetic demo content written for TracePay; the analyst and the interviews are fictional.

Across 38 interviews with engineering leaders in Q3 2026, the most common reason for expanding assistant licences was review throughput rather than code generation. Teams reported that assistants shortened pull-request review cycles by a median of 27%.

Procurement has shifted from individual seats to platform agreements. Twenty-two of the 38 organisations had moved to an enterprise agreement that bundles the assistant with code search and policy controls, up from nine in the previous round.

The biggest blocker to wider rollout is provenance. Thirty-one leaders said legal teams now ask which licensed sources and models contributed to generated code or documentation, and most could not answer without manual reconstruction.

Leaders expect pricing pressure in 2027. Most interviewees anticipate a move from per-seat pricing to usage-based pricing tied to accepted suggestions, and several already negotiate caps on monthly usage charges.

Smaller studios are using assistants to produce paid deliverables such as research briefs and audit reports. These teams worry most about licensing: they combine several paid data sources and lack a reliable way to prove which sources a deliverable used.
