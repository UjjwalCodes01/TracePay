Northwind DevTools Spend Index, 2026 edition. All figures are synthetic demo data produced for TracePay and describe no real company. The index tracks software-development tooling spend across 1,200 mid-size engineering organisations, reported in quarterly waves.

Total tracked developer-tools spend reached $41.8 billion annualised in Q3 2026, up 14.2% year over year. Growth accelerated from 11.6% in Q3 2025, driven mainly by AI-assisted coding and observability purchases.

AI coding assistants were the fastest-growing category, with annualised spend of $6.9 billion in Q3 2026, up 61% year over year. Assistant seats now represent 16.5% of all tooling spend, compared with 11.7% a year earlier.

Observability and monitoring spend rose 18% year over year to $8.4 billion. Organisations adopting AI coding assistants increased observability budgets 1.4 times faster than non-adopters, which the index attributes to higher deployment frequency.

CI/CD and build infrastructure spend grew only 4.1% to $7.2 billion, the slowest of the major categories. Respondents cited consolidation onto fewer platforms and the migration of build minutes to cheaper self-hosted runners.

Security tooling for the software supply chain grew 22% to $3.1 billion. Spend on dependency scanning and software bill of materials (SBOM) tools doubled among organisations subject to procurement attestation requirements.

Average tooling spend per engineer was $11,400 in Q3 2026, up from $10,300 a year earlier. Organisations with more than 500 engineers spent 23% less per engineer than smaller organisations, reflecting volume discounts.
